Choosing a vape detector for your school requires evaluating three things: false alarm rate in real bathrooms (not lab conditions), total installed cost (not just hardware price), and whether staff will still trust it after the first month. Most schools buy on features and regret it within weeks when the sensor fires on cologne for the fifth time.

## Skip the Feature Lists. Ask These Questions.

Every vape detector company has a feature page that makes their product sound perfect. But the features that matter are not the ones that show up in marketing material. They are the ones that determine whether the sensor is still being used six months after installation.

Here are the questions that separate a good purchase from a regret.

## Question 1: What Is the False Alarm Rate in Real Bathrooms?

This is the single most important question. A vape detector that fires on cologne, cleaning spray, or deodorant will lose staff trust within the first week. Once trust is gone, the sensor is useless regardless of how accurate it is at detecting actual vape.

**What to ask the vendor:**
- Has this sensor been tested in environments where body spray and cleaning products are used?
- Can you show me data on false positive rates from real school deployments?
- How does the sensor distinguish between vape aerosol and deodorant spray?

**Red flags:** If the vendor cannot answer these questions with specifics, or if they redirect to "adjustable sensitivity settings," the sensor likely uses threshold-based detection that will produce false alarms.

## Question 2: What Does Installation Actually Require?

Marketing says "easy install." Reality says "call the electrician."

**What to ask:**
- Does this sensor require Power over Ethernet?
- Does it need Wi-Fi, and has it been tested in concrete bathrooms?
- Can a custodian install it, or does it require a licensed contractor?
- What is the typical timeline from purchase to working sensor?

If the answer involves cable runs, network provisioning, or professional installation, budget 3-6 weeks and $500+ per sensor location on top of the hardware cost.

## Question 3: What Are the Ongoing Costs?

The purchase price is not the total cost.

**What to ask:**
- Is there a monthly or annual license fee?
- Is cloud access included, or is that a separate subscription?
- What does the annual maintenance or support contract cost?
- Are firmware updates included or paid?

Some sensors cost $300 to buy and $500/year to subscribe to. Over 3 years, the "cheap" sensor costs more than the one with a higher sticker price and no subscription.

## Question 4: What Happens When the Network Goes Down?

School networks go down. Wi-Fi access points fail. Switches reboot. Power outages happen.

**What to ask:**
- Does the sensor continue to detect and log events when the network is unavailable?
- How does it reconnect after an outage?
- Does it depend on the school's Wi-Fi or does it have its own connectivity?

A sensor that depends on school Wi-Fi in a concrete bathroom is a sensor that will have connectivity issues.

## Question 5: What Data Does It Collect?

Parents and school boards are increasingly sensitive to monitoring technology in schools. Understand exactly what the sensor captures.

**What to ask:**
- Does it record audio?
- Does it have a camera or any imaging capability?
- What data is transmitted to the cloud?
- Where is the data stored, and who has access?
- Is there student-identifiable information in the data?

A sensor that monitors air quality only is a very different conversation with parents than a sensor from a camera/surveillance company.

## Question 6: Can I See It Work Before I Buy?

Any vendor confident in their product should let you test it.

**What to ask:**
- Can I get a demo unit for my school?
- Is there a trial period?
- What happens after the trial: am I locked into a subscription?

Be cautious of "free trials" that convert to expensive annual contracts. Understand the full terms before agreeing.

## The Practical Checklist

When evaluating vape detectors, score each option on these criteria:

| Criteria | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| False alarm rate | Critical | Ask for real deployment data, not lab results |
| Installation complexity | High | Can a custodian do it, or do you need a contractor? |
| Total 3-year cost | High | Include all subscriptions, licenses, infrastructure |
| Battery vs. wired | High | Does your building have PoE in bathrooms? |
| Connectivity method | Medium | Cellular > Wi-Fi for bathroom environments |
| Privacy posture | Medium | No cameras/mics matters for parent buy-in |
| Vendor lock-in | Medium | Can you switch without losing infrastructure investment? |
