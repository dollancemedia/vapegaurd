## The Short Version

HALO is a wired, PoE-powered multi-threat sensor that detects vape, THC, noise, and air quality. It has been on the market since 2018 and is installed in thousands of schools.

Mistio is a battery-powered vape detector built specifically for K-12 bathrooms. It runs for a full year on a single battery, installs in under a minute, and uses AI trained to ignore cologne, cleaning spray, and deodorant.

| Feature | Mistio | HALO |
|---|---|---|
| Power | Battery (1 year) | PoE (requires ethernet cable) |
| Install time | Under 1 minute | Hours to days (electrician + IT) |
| Wi-Fi required | No (cellular) | Yes |
| False alarm rate | Near zero (AI-filtered) | Commonly reported by schools |
| Cologne/deodorant filtering | Trained specifically on these | Threshold-based detection |
| Monthly fees | None | License + cloud subscription |

## The False Alarm Problem

The number one complaint schools have about HALO is false alarms. A student sprays Axe body spray in the bathroom, the HALO fires an alert, a staff member walks over, finds nothing. This happens three, four, five times. Then the staff stops responding. At that point, you have a $1,000+ sensor on the wall that nobody trusts.

Mistio's AI was trained on hundreds of real-world samples of cologne, deodorant, cleaning products, and hair spray. It learned what vape aerosol actually looks like at a molecular level compared to everything else that gets sprayed in a bathroom. When Mistio alerts, staff responds, because they know it is real.

## Installation: Weeks vs. Minutes

HALO requires Power over Ethernet. That means running an ethernet cable from a network switch to the sensor location, usually through a ceiling or wall. Most school bathrooms do not have ethernet drops. That means:

- An IT ticket to provision a network port
- An electrician to run cable through the ceiling
- A contractor to patch drywall or ceiling tiles
- Weeks of scheduling before a single sensor goes live

Mistio mounts with two screws. No cables. No ceiling access. No IT involvement. A custodian can install five sensors during a lunch break.

## Connectivity in Concrete Bathrooms

School bathrooms are the hardest rooms to get Wi-Fi signal into. Concrete walls, metal stall dividers, and ceramic tile all block wireless signals. HALO requires a stable network connection to send alerts. If the Wi-Fi drops, which it does in bathrooms, the sensor goes dark.

Mistio uses cellular connectivity. It does not depend on school Wi-Fi. Concrete walls, metal stalls, basement bathrooms: Mistio stays connected through all of it.

## Total Cost of Ownership

HALO's sticker price is only part of the cost. Add in:

- Electrician fees for cable runs ($200-500 per sensor location)
- Network switch upgrades if ports are full
- IT staff time for configuration
- Annual cloud/license subscription fees
- Ongoing maintenance when cables get damaged

Mistio has no infrastructure costs. No license fees. No cloud subscription. One sensor, one price. Replace the battery once a year.

## When HALO Makes Sense

HALO is a reasonable choice if you already have PoE infrastructure in every bathroom, you want multi-threat detection (noise, gunshot, vaping in one device), and you have dedicated IT staff to manage the network.

## When Mistio Is the Better Fit

Mistio is the better choice if you need sensors in bathrooms without ethernet, you are tired of false alarms from body spray, you do not have the budget or timeline for infrastructure work, or you want to deploy across an entire building in a single day.

## Bottom Line

HALO was one of the first vape detectors on the market, and it brought awareness to the problem. But schools keep reporting the same issues: false alarms from body spray, expensive installs, and Wi-Fi dead zones in bathrooms. Mistio was built to solve those exact problems. Battery power, cellular connectivity, and AI that actually knows the difference between vape and cologne.
